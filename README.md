## 📄 Abstract
In the last 25 years, the scholarly discussion of chronic Lyme disease (CLD) and post-treatment
Lyme disease syndrome (PTLDS) has been characterized by scientific dispute, patient activism, and
media impact. This study rigorously examines more than 8,800 scholarly abstracts from 2000 to
2024, utilizing sophisticated AI approaches, such as position detection and thematic analysis, to
delineate the progression of viewpoints in the literature. Our data indicate a notable transition from
infection-centric models to immune-mediated explanations, with papers supporting PTLDS becoming
more common in high-impact medical journals. However, research centered on CLD continues
to dominate broader, hypothesis-driven publications. Identified key themes include diagnostic
complexity, therapeutic conflicts, patient-centered experiences, and sociocultural influences affecting
discourse. The data highlight ongoing diagnostic ambiguity, an increasing focus on immunological
dysregulation, and the relegation of bacterial persistence studies. Journal specialty and editorial
biases profoundly affect discourse, shaping public opinions and therapeutic guidelines. The study
advises governments to ensure equitable research funding and establish consistent diagnostic criteria.
Clinicians are encouraged to implement patient-centered care strategies that recognize the validity of
enduring symptoms, regardless of the disputed diagnosis. Communication strategists must prioritize
connecting scientific consensus with patient experiences through clear and empathic communication.
This thorough analysis of scholarly discussions offers essential insights into the structural dynamics
of Lyme disease debates, providing a framework for enhancing health communication tactics for
disputed illnesses and promoting better-informed, compassionate healthcare practices.
